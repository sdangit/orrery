▸ Tutorial

Out here, the box intuition is true. Every system is a fixed point in one galactic grid, measured in light years with Sol at 0,0,0.

X runs galactic east–west. Z points toward the galactic core. Y is height above the galactic plane — the flat grid below.

The vertical stalks are the trick: they drop each star onto the plane, exactly like the in-game galaxy map. Read a position as "where on the floor, plus how high."

The cyan sphere is your jump range. Routing is hop-by-hop through whatever stars fall inside it — this is where the traveling-salesman feel comes from. Widen it and watch the plotted route need fewer hops.

Inside a system the box breaks. Nothing holds still: planets sweep along orbits, stations ride along with them. Your destination is a moving point.

In supercruise your max speed is choked by gravity wells. The dim rings show them — watch the ship's speed collapse whenever its path skims a planet.

That's why a station 1,200 ls out can cost more time than three extra hyperspace jumps, and why cutting close past a big planet is slower than swinging wide.

Launch the ship at different targets and time scales. Watch SPEED vs DIST in the readout — the deceleration wall on approach is the same one you fight in the game.
